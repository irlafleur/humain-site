9. The Power of 1%: Compounding, Honestly

The maths of getting 1% better every day, the honest limits of the idea, and two agents (Daily 1% Review and the 5-Minute Task Killer) to practise it.

You'll leave with: the real maths of compounding, an honest view of what “1% better” means, and a nightly routine you can run in five minutes.

The maths

If something grows by 1% each day, you multiply by 1.01 each day. Here's what that does (we calculated these ourselves; you can check them on any calculator):

Days1.01 ^ days0.99 ^ days
30about 1.35×about 0.74×
100about 2.70×about 0.37×
365about 37.8×about 0.03×

Compounding means you earn gains on your earlier gains. It's the same idea banks use for interest: with a hypothetical $1,000 at a hypothetical 7% a year for 30 years, simple interest gives about $3,100 total while compounding gives about $7,612. (Illustration only: not a real rate, not financial advice.) And it works in reverse too: improving 1% a day looks like growth; slipping 1% a day looks like decay.

The honest part

You will not become 38 times more productiveSkills and habits don't grow like a savings account, and a real “1%” is hard to measure. The 1% idea is a way of thinking: small, consistent improvements beat rare big efforts, and small daily leaks (wasted time, repeated errors) add up too. Use it as a lens, not a promise.

What does a “1% improvement” look like?

Not a number: a tiny change you can name. Examples:

  • Turn off notifications for the first 25 minutes of work.
  • Save a prompt you retype often.
  • Create a template for an email you send weekly.
  • Check your numbers once before sending.
  • Learn one keyboard shortcut.

Things that stay (templates, shortcuts, saved prompts) compound. Things you have to redo every day don't.

Two agents to practise with

1. Daily 1% Review (evening, 5 minutes)

The agent asks four questions and gives you one win, one 1% improvement, one thing to automate, and three tasks for tomorrow. Get it from the Agent Library.

2. 5-Minute Task Killer (when you're stuck)

Slices a daunting task into steps of five minutes or less, with the easiest first. Get it from the Agent Library. Quick version:

Break this task into steps that each take 5 minutes or less: [TASK]. Number them, start each with a verb, give minutes per step, and make step 1 take under 2 minutes.

Exercise

A 7-day experiment.

  1. Pick one thing to improve by 1% this week (not five).
  2. Each evening, run the Daily 1% Review. Write the “1% improvement” in your prompt log or a note.
  3. On day 7, count how many improvements stayed (templates, saved prompts, habits) and how many you had to redo.
  4. Write one sentence: what compounds for me?

Check the maths: use a calculator to compute 1.01 to the power of 7, then 1.01 to the power of 30. How far is a week from a month?

Key takeaways

  • 1% a day compounds: roughly 1.35× in 30 days and 37.8× in 365 (the maths, not a promise).
  • The idea is a lens: small, kept improvements beat rare heroic efforts.
  • Things that stay (templates, saved prompts) compound; things you redo don't.
